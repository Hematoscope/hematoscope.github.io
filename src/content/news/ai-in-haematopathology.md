Haematopathology — the study of blood diseases through microscopic examination — has long relied on the trained eye of an expert. Identifying a blast cell, flagging an abnormal neutrophil, or quantifying a differential count requires years of experience and sustained concentration. AI is now beginning to assist in this process, not to replace the pathologist, but to make their work faster, more consistent, and more scalable.

## The Challenge of Scale

A busy haematology laboratory can process hundreds of blood film reviews per day. Each one involves scanning a slide, identifying relevant cells, and making a differential count. This is time-consuming, and fatigue introduces variability. The same slide reviewed at 8am and 4pm may yield subtly different results.

AI systems trained on large, annotated datasets can perform this counting task continuously and consistently, flagging cases that warrant closer human attention and allowing pathologists to focus their expertise where it matters most.
